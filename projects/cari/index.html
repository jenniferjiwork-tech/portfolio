02

The problem

Each tool stays organized. The project between them doesn’t.

On a launch, everyone works in their own lane and their own tool — Figma, Sheets, Drive, Slack, email. When something changes in one, the others don’t always hear about it.

In my campaign, editorial and digital work, keeping a project aligned took constant coordination. Another workspace would mean more to maintain.

Solution · 02

Checking decisions

When direction changes, Cari shows what else may need updating.

Step 1

A change becomes important

A Creative Direction update arrives from Google Slides.

Cari notices it followed a creative review and suggests it may be a key decision.

Step 2

A person confirms what matters

Sofia saves it as a key decision: “Shift to natural light and product-led layouts.”

AI can suggest a change; a person confirms the decision.

Step 3

Cari checks the related project sources

The confirmed decision links to its related sources and highlights files that may still reflect the earlier direction — here, the Styling board, last updated before the change.

Step 4

The team confirms what still holds

Cari flags the Styling board because the creative direction changed after it was last updated.

Open the file, mark it still current, or mark it as not affected.

Why it matters

Cari surfaces the possible mismatch. The team decides whether anything actually needs changing.

07

Reflection

Less coordination. More room for creative work.

Cari grew out of my experience in creative teams and conversations with others facing similar challenges. When everyone is focused on their own work, changes, approvals and handoffs can become difficult to keep track of—especially without someone dedicated to coordinating the project.

​

I started by imagining one workspace to manage everything. Through the research and design process, I shifted toward supporting the tools teams already use. The most important decision became where automation should stop: Cari can flag a possible mismatch, but people need to confirm what a decision means and whether anything needs changing.

​

The concept still needs testing with teams. I would want to understand whether the updates are useful, the flags are clear, and Cari reduces coordination rather than becoming another tool to maintain. The integrations remain conceptual.
